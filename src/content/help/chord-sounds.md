The Highway can play the chords as well as the metronome, so a group hears the
harmony they're aiming at. Chords can come from a built-in tone the app
generates on the spot, or from recordings you make of your own instrument.

## Before you start

- The song needs beat timing, the same as the [Highway
  view](/help/highway-view) itself. If it has none, tag it first — see
  [Tag the beats of a song](/help/tag-beats).
- To record your own chords you need a microphone and a browser that will let
  the app use it. Everything else works without one.

## Steps

1. Open a song, tap **Perform**, and choose **Highway** from the view menu.
2. Tap the sliders button in the bottom bar, then turn on **Play the chords**.
3. Choose where the sound comes from under **Sound**:
   - **Built-in tone** — generated by the app, so every chord in every key
     already works.
   - **My recordings** — your own audio (see *Record your own chords* below).
     Any chord you haven't recorded falls back to the built-in tone.
4. For the built-in tone, pick a **Voicing** — **Root note** (just the bass
   note) or **Full chord** — and a **Tone**: **Pluck**, **Strings**, or
   **Organ**.
5. Pick **When** the chord sounds:
   - **On the beat** — one hit as you reach the chord.
   - **Held** — sounds on its beat and holds until the next chord.
   - **Every beat** — re-struck on every beat until the next chord.
6. Set **Chord volume** to balance the chords against the click.
7. Press the large **Play** button to hear it.

## Put a break in a song

Place **N.C.** on a beat where the chords should stop. It silences whatever is
sounding until the next chord, in all three timings — so held and repeated
chords can still have rests. Add one from the Highway's **Edit** mode: tap
**＋ Chord**, click the grid where the break starts, and type `N.C.` See
[Edit chords and lyrics on the beat grid](/help/edit-on-the-grid).

## Record your own chords

1. Go to **Settings → Chord sounds**.
2. Under **Record a chord**, type the chord name (`G`, `Am7`, …). You have to
   name it before you can record — the name is how the sound gets matched to the
   chords in your songs.
3. Tap **Record**. The first time, your browser asks to use the microphone —
   allow it. (If you say no by mistake, the app tells you so; re-allow it in your
   browser's site settings and try again.)
4. Play the chord once on your instrument, then tap **Stop** — the button counts
   the seconds as it records. The recording saves on its own.
5. Use the play button in the list to check it, or the bin to delete it.

A recording is filed under the chord name, so one recording of `G` plays for
every `G` in every song. Recording a chord you already have replaces the old
take (the app warns you first). You can record **N.C.** too, if you want a sound
on the break.

Remember to set **Sound** to **My recordings** — the chip shows how many you
have. Recordings travel with your backups, so they come back with everything
else when you restore.

## What you'll see

Chords sound exactly on their beat, locked to the same clock as the metronome,
and the chord at the playhead lights up in your accent colour as it plays. If
you transpose the song, the sound transposes with it — a song moved up two
semitones plays (and looks for a recording of) the chord you can actually hear.

Settings are saved, so the chords sound the same way next time.
