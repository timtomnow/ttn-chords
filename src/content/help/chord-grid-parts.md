Some parts are a chord pattern rather than words: an intro, a solo, or "just keep
going round G–C for eight bars". For those, write the part as a **chord grid** —
one line of bars, the way you'd put it on a whiteboard. The app works out exactly
where each chord lands, so a grid part never needs beat tagging.

## Before you start

- Turn on [authoring (admin) mode](/help/turn-on-admin-mode).
- Set the song's **Time** (e.g. `4/4`), since that's what decides how long a bar
  is.

## Steps

1. Open a song and tap **Edit**.
2. In the part you want, tap **Grid** in the header (next to **Lyrics**).
3. Type the progression, using `|` between bars:
   - `| G | C |` — one chord per bar.
   - `| G C |` — two chords, half a bar each.
   - `| G . . C |` — four slots, so G lands on beat 1 and C on beat 4.
   - `|: G | C :| x4` — play those two bars four times.
   - `| G | % |` — repeat the previous bar. `| - |` is a bar of rest.
4. Check the bars listed on the right, then tap **Done**.

Tapping a chord in the palette row appends it to the grid, which saves typing the
symbols out.

## What you'll see

The right-hand pane lists the bars the grid makes and the total bar count, and
tells you the part needs no tagging. The chords appear on the **Highway** view at
exactly those beats straight away.

Switching a part that already has chords into **Grid** moves those chords into the
grid text so nothing is lost — but from then on the grid is where that part's
chords live. Its lyrics are kept and shown beneath the chord line.
